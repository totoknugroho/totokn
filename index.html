the question
How can science objectively measure what the human mind silently endures?

That question started at home, not in a lab. Growing up, I watched my mother live with epilepsy — seizures that seemed to arrive from nowhere, but were often preceded by things no one else could see: fatigue building for days, stress she carried quietly. I wanted a way to make those unseen factors visible before they became a crisis.

That's the thread running through everything below: from an undergraduate thesis on stress detection, to a master's spent inside EEG labs in Tokyo, to a PhD that asks the same question at a larger scale — using brain signals and machine learning to make mental and neurological health something we can measure, not just guess at.

What I work on

My research sits at the intersection of neuroengineering, signal processing, and machine learning — using EEG to find robust, reproducible markers of chronic stress and, more broadly, mental and neurological states that don't show up on a checklist.

  • 01EEG acquisition & experimental design — from electrode placement to protocol, including cognitive tasks like the Stroop test
  • 02Signal processing & feature extraction for chronic stress and cognitive load markers
  • 03Machine learning models (including SVM) applied to physiological and neural signals
  • 04Literature synthesis and manuscript development in computational neuroscience
